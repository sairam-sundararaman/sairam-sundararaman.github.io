Why your DP-SGD budget burns faster than you think

November 2026 · No. 4 in the privacy series · 9 min

The promise of the moments accountant is that composition cost grows like \(O(\sqrt{T})\), not linearly.1 But in practice people set the noise multiplier once, train for longer than planned, and are surprised when \(\varepsilon\) at epoch 40 dwarfs their estimate. The accounting is right; the mental model is wrong.

1 Abadi et al., 2016 [1]. The accountant bounds Rényi divergence per step and composes multiplicatively before converting to (ε, δ).

Consider one epoch of sampling rate \(q = B/n\). Per-step privacy loss under zCDP is roughly:

\(\rho(q,\sigma) \;=\; \tfrac{q^2}{2\sigma^2} \;+\; O(q^4)\)
(1)

Composing \(T\) steps gives \(\rho_{\text{tot}} = T\rho\), so the sqrt-law comes from converting Rényi to approximate-DP, not from composition itself.2 Double \(T\) and you double \(\rho_{\text{tot}}\) — the square root only appears against \(\delta\).

2 This conflation is the single most common bug I've seen in training scripts. See §3 of [2] for the tight conversion.

A two-line audit you can run tonight

# log the true spend every checkpoint, not just epochs
def eps_now(T):  # analytic Gaussian mechanism
    rho = T * q**2 / (2 * sigma**2)
    return rho + 2*sigma*q*math.sqrt(rho * math.log(1/delta))

What to do about it

Budget in steps, not epochs [1]. Log \(\varepsilon\) at every checkpoint. If you plan early stopping, the planned horizon must be the accounting horizon — restarts silently re-spend.
